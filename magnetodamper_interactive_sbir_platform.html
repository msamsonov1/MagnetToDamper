Electrodynamic, Permeability, and Structural Governing Equations

The MagnetoDamper operates at the intersection of non-linear magnetic saturation dynamics, Lorentz electromagnetic counter-force generation, and structural finite-element modal coupling in space telescope optical benches.

1. Lorentz Damping & Dynamic Differential Inductance

Active voice-coil actuators generate an electromagnetic damping counter-force $F_{\text{act}}$ proportional to current $i(t)$, flux density $B$, and effective coil conductor length $l$. However, coil displacement $x(t)$ and saturation flux compress the incremental magnetic permeability $\mu_{\Delta}$:

$$F_{\text{act}}(t) = B(x) \cdot l \cdot i(t) = -c_d \dot{x}(t) - k_a x(t)$$

The coil self-inductance $L$ is a function of the dynamic reluctance $\mathcal{R}_m(x, B)$ of the magnetic circuit. As the core approaches saturation ($B \to B_{\text{sat}}$), the relative permeability $\mu_r(H) = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\frac{dB}{dH}$ drops dramatically:

$$L(x, i) = \frac{N^2}{\mathcal{R}_{\text{gap}}(x) + \frac{l_{\text{core}}}{\mu_0 \mu_r(H) A_{\text{core}}}} \approx \frac{L_0}{1 + \alpha x(t) + \beta \left(\frac{i}{I_{\text{sat}}}\right)^2}$$
Coupling: $N = 340 \text{ turns}$, $B_0 = 1.15 \text{ T}$, $I_{\text{sat}} = 2.45 \text{ A}$, $L_0 = 22.40 \text{ mH}$.

2. Microscopic Domain Alignment & Permeability Collapse

Ferromagnetic pole pieces (Hiperco-50A alloy) rely on spontaneous magnetic domain wall motion. Once the external magnetic excitation $H$ forces complete domain rotation along the easy axis, magnetization $M$ plateaus at the spontaneous limit $M_s$:

$$B(H) = \mu_0 \left( H + M_s \tanh\left(\frac{H}{H_k}\right) \right) \implies \lim_{H \to \infty} \frac{dB}{dH} = \mu_0$$

When $\mu_{\Delta} \to \mu_0$, the magnetic core behaves identically to vacuum. The differential inductance gradient with respect to applied force collapses to zero:

$$\left.\frac{\partial L}{\partial F}\right|_{\text{saturated}} \longrightarrow 0 \quad \text{while} \quad \left.\frac{\partial^2 L}{\partial F^2}\right|_{\text{knee}} \ll 0$$
Material: Hiperco-50A ($49\%\text{ Fe}, 49\%\text{ Co}, 2\%\text{ V}$) | $B_{\text{sat}} = 2.35 \text{ T}$ vacuum-annealed.

3. High-Frequency Ripple Observer & Dynamic Inversion

Rather than installing fragile displacement proximity sensors inside the cryogenic optical bench envelope, the system injects an ultra-low-amplitude $10\text{ kHz}$ sinusoidal dither $v_{\text{probe}}(t) = V_d \sin(\omega_p t)$ onto the voice-coil winding:

$$Z_{\text{coil}}(\omega_p) = R_{\text{coil}} + j\omega_p L(x, i) \implies L_{\text{meas}} = \frac{1}{\omega_p}\sqrt{\left|\frac{V_d}{I_p}\right|^2 - R_{\text{coil}}^2}$$

A recursive Kalman filter tracks the $(F, L)$ trajectory. An imminent bottoming-out metric $\Psi_{\text{sat}}$ is defined by the curvature of the trajectory in phase space:

$$\Psi_{\text{sat}}(t) = 1.0 - \frac{L(t) - L_{\text{min}}}{L_{\text{nominal}} - L_{\text{min}}} \cdot \left[ 1 + \left| \frac{d^2 L}{d F^2} \right| \right]^{-1} \ge \Psi_{\text{crit}} = 0.82$$
Observer Latency: $120\ \mu\text{s}$ at $10\text{ kHz}$ injection | Zero added optical-bench harnesses.

4. Telescope Jitter Slew & Wheel Harmonic Shift

Reaction wheel dynamic imbalance imparts centrifugal harmonic excitation forces at wheel angular frequency $\Omega_{\text{wheel}}$ and subharmonic multiples $h_k \in \{1.0, 2.0, 5.2\}$:

$$F_{\text{dist}}(t) = \sum_{k} U_{d,k} \cdot (h_k \Omega_{\text{wheel}})^2 \cos(h_k \Omega_{\text{wheel}} t + \phi_k)$$
